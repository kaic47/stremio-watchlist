# Kai's film taste

This is the single source of truth for what Kai likes. Every recommendation thread reads it first and writes
Kai's feedback back into it before finishing (see [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)). Keep each entry to one line with the
reason, and use Kai's own words where possible. Date anything that can go stale.

**Status (2026-11-16):** first real profile, from Kai's own answers to a taste questionnaire. Still open: subtitles,
runtime, recent ratings (see Open questions).

## How to weigh evidence

1. **What Kai says directly**, about a film or a type of film, with a reason. This outranks everything else.
2. **Verdicts given after watching**: `watched "<title>" --rating N --note "..."` (source `manual` in
   `history.json`), and reactions to recommendation lists.
3. **Imported history** (`history.json` entries whose source is `letterboxd`, `imdb` or `trakt`). Weak signal: Kai has
   "only rated a few movies somewhat randomly". Use it mainly as the seen list, so you don't recommend what Kai has
   already watched. Treat a rating there as a hint only when it is extreme (5, or 1 and below), and never let it
   override 1 or 2.

Accepting a list of picks ("these are fantastic, add them all") means the pitch landed, not that Kai has seen and
liked the films. Don't promote picks to Loves until Kai has watched and rated them.

## The short version

Kai wants films that **feel real, inspiring and grounded**, with **great dialogue** and **a great soundtrack**. Kai also
loves **adventure**, **sharp satire and absurd comedy**, and **the canon**: "really interested in watching all of the best
ones ever". Culturally relevant beats niche. Sad is fine if it means something. Being made by music people,
or about music, is a strong plus.

## About Kai

- Professional electronic music producer (bass music) and touring musician. Films about musicians, youth, and
  figuring it out hit close to home.
- Very open-minded about concepts. Not usually after niche arthouse: "things that are culturally relevant are often
  more impressive to me because of their ability to be so. It's hard to do."
- Loves films that teach something or grow a passion: "anything that helps me understand something or learn or grow
  a passion is often great."
- Soundtrack and composer matter a lot. Joe Hisaishi is a favourite composer. Mac Miller is a favourite musician, and Kai
  likes the Eternal Sunshine link: Jon Brion scored it and also worked on Mac Miller's *Swimming* and *Circles*.
- Mostly films, but loves great prestige TV and comedy shows.
- Wants terse, blunt recommendation lists, one line per pick with the reason.

## What makes a film land (Kai's reasons, by theme)

- **Real, relatable, grounded human stories** about relationships, without being sappy rom-coms ("I don't even mind sappy").
  Good Will Hunting, The Perks of Being a Wallflower ("felt really real"), 10 Things I Hate About You.
- **Inspiring and perseverance stories.** Good Will Hunting, Lion ("an extraordinary tale of perseverance"), Tick, Tick... Boom!
- **Witty, sharp dialogue.** Good Will Hunting: "the dialogue is insane."
- **Great soundtrack.** Called out for Good Will Hunting, Almost Famous, Eternal Sunshine, and Hisaishi's Miyazaki scores.
- **Youth and figuring it out, music-scene romance.** Almost Famous: band drama and the touring life, "romanticizes music in a beautiful way".
- **Adventure.** "Any kind of adventure movie is awesome." "Adventure tales, huge W." Lord of the Rings, The Goonies.
- **Fun 80s and 90s high-school films**, which Kai has been into lately. Ferris Bueller's Day Off, 10 Things I Hate About You.
- **Nostalgia and comfort.** Harry Potter (read all the books as a kid), Miyazaki.
- **Surreal, magical, well-meant art.** Miyazaki: "you can tell he's really well intended with his art", and Kai likes his strong female leads.
- **Sad but meaningful, with an interesting concept.** Eternal Sunshine: "one of the saddest movies I've ever seen... really struck a chord."
- **Great backstories.** Good Will Hunting being written by two college kids is part of the appeal.

## Comedy

- **Satire and cringe stunts on real people:** Borat ("genius... fooled all those people", "made fun of everyone around
  him without them noticing"), Nathan For You and everything Nathan Fielder has made.
- **Absurd, "fried" humour:** I Think You Should Leave.
- **Animated satire:** South Park (also impressed by the creators' independence), Rick and Morty "during its prime".
- **Will Ferrell classics:** Step Brothers is "one of the funniest movies ever", but that style is "not usually what I'm
  looking for anymore".
- Kai wants good new comedy and finds it hard to come by ("comedy lately has been slacking"). A real gap to fill.

## Dislikes and soft avoids

- **Mad Max: Fury Road** (2015): "didn't like that much because there's basically no plot and it's just like action, pretty boring to me. Didn't feel like a lot of human consequences." (Inference: wall-to-wall action without story or human stakes doesn't land.) *(2026-11-15)*
- **Musicals as a form** (characters singing the story). Films about musicians are welcome. *(2026-11-15)*
- **Niche arthouse**: not a hard no, but "I don't typically want" it.
- **Really old films** (pre-80s): "not a huge fan of like really old shit", though Kai sometimes appreciates them for their period. 80s are "okay sometimes", 90s "usually okay".
- **Anime as a genre**: "not particularly into anime... not against getting into it", but "it might not be often what I'm looking for." Miyazaki is the exception.
- Kai can't think of many widely loved films they disliked.

## Dealbreakers

None. "I have no hard no's as far as tone." *(2026-11-16)*
